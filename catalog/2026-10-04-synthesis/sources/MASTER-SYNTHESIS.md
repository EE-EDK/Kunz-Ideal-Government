## Axioms

Eight fixed starting points. Every decision below must trace back to them, and none is open for debate.

| Axiom | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| Natural rights | Objective natural law exists. Rights are recognized, not granted. Defensible equally through deism, Christianity, or secular reason. |
| Soft determinism | Agency is real. Physics constrains but does not fully determine. Hard determinism is rejected. |
| Hierarchy | Dominance hierarchy is a biological fact of the species, not a cultural artifact. Flat structures are unstable. The design works with hierarchy, not against it. |
| Non-aggression (NAP) | The Non-Aggression Principle is the default norm. Voluntary arrangements first; coercion only where structurally necessary. |
| Subsidiarity | Power dispersed is power constrained. Decisions are made at the lowest competent level. The Swiss canton model is the closest real-world analog. |
| Anti-metropolitanism | Urban density degrades behavior at scale. Rural interdependence produces better outcomes. The design structurally limits density. |
| Children as protected class | Children hold rights in trust. NAP applies asymmetrically until capacity is established. The extra protection is constitutional, not discretionary. |
| AI as legitimacy tool | AI checks law for contradiction, maintains parity numbers, and supports data-driven fairness. It is infrastructure, not governance. |

**Working principles added during the sessions** (settled, same standing as the axioms for process purposes):

- The constitutional layer is additive only. Nothing is ever removed from it.
- Resolved items are not reopened without an explicit instruction from the owner.
- Any loose end a decision creates is logged and queued, never silently dropped.
- Merge what can be merged. Simplicity and non-redundancy are primary goals of the whole system (2026-09-13).
- AI is always contestable and is audited only when contested. It takes on a governing role only where it fills a bureaucratic hole and proves long-run usefulness, and it can be phased out if it draws too many challenges (2026-09-13).

## Cells, citizenship, and the coordination body

The system is a body made of cells: self-governing units modeled on Swiss cantons, joined under one constitution.

**Cells**

- Each cell is named (for example "Iron-Cell", "FreeFarming-Cell") and has its own constitution, subordinate to the body constitution.
- Geography is resource-balanced at founding, so no cell holds a monopoly on a high-value natural resource.
- Cells compete for citizens on birth accounts, resources, and quality of governance.
- A struggling cell is not bailed out or dissolved. Other cells make competing absorption offers, subject to the 90% dual vote and the accumulation ceiling, which stops one dominant cell from absorbing everything.

**Citizenship**

- Every citizen holds two statuses: body citizenship (portable everywhere) and cell citizenship (specific to one cell).
- Body citizenship requires clearing a universal minimum that no cell can lower.
- After clearing it, the applicant chooses a cell and must be accepted by it. The cell may add its own requirements above the floor.
- A lenient cell therefore cannot become a back door: it can grant only its own cell citizenship.
- The tier structure is settled and not to be reopened.

**Coordination body** (framework settled; numbers still open under 2D)

- Hybrid membership: rotating cell delegates plus equal representation from technocratic sectors.
- Size scales with population.
- A new cell or an absorption triggers a rebalancing of seats.

**Protection against oligarchy** (settled, not to be reopened). Three checks in order:

1. Resource-balanced cell geography at founding.
2. The body-level progressive contribution equation, which scales with value and applies in every cell.
3. Cell-level anti-monopoly rules for non-resource industries.

## Layer 1: Foundation

The dispute process is designed; who may sit as an arbiter, what the body-level process may hear, the NAP edge cases, and enforcement against a defiant cell are still open.

### 1A: Rights arbitration (mostly resolved)

How a rights dispute is decided, in order:

1. Both parties independently submit a structured statement: their rights claim paired with a factual record. The record is assembled by retrieval from sources and filtered by a judiciary committee.
2. AI reads the statements against the constitutional text and applies a published evidentiary rubric. It issues a recommended ruling.
3. Either party may reject it and escalate, with counsel, to a committee drawn by lot (sortition). That committee is bound by the same rubric.
4. A rotating panel of human arbiters gives the final ruling. The panel audits only decisions that are contested, never a random sample.

Rules that apply throughout:

- Rulings rest on constitutional text only. No "spirit of the law" reasoning.
- No precedent accumulates. Each case is decided fresh against the text.
- Disputes about facts are routed to trial rather than arbitration.

**Glossary (resolved 2026-06-28).** The constitution carries a plain-language glossary of key terms (force, initiation, citizen, cell, rights, and so on) as part of its immutable core. It is derived at founding and fixed. Changing a definition requires a threshold higher than the normal amendment process; the exact number is deferred to 2D. Clarifying notes may be attached as annotations but cannot change a definition. No body may redefine a term through any lighter process.

### 1B: NAP edge cases (open, not started)

Four questions, each needing either a constitutional answer or a defined route to arbitration:

- Fraud: taking without consent but without physical force. Is it a NAP violation?
- Cross-cell environmental damage: force against whom?
- Psychological coercion: does it count as initiating force?
- Negligence: unintended harm. Still a violation?

### 1C: Enforcement hierarchy (open, not started)

What happens when a cell violates the body constitution and refuses to comply. The coordination body has no enforcement arm defined. This item now includes everything that was 6B:

- The mechanism against a rogue cell and the definition of the enforcement arm
- Economic sanctions
- The threshold for authorizing military force
- Isolation or loss of representation

## Layer 3: Economic

The economic floor and funding rules are already fixed in the constitution; the four layer items themselves are all open.

**Already decided (from the constitutional layer):**

- Contribution is a percentage of value, reviewed every 10 years, with no default income tax (c15).
- Every business, industry, and service carries a parity number: a dynamic, public value metric set by technocratic sector owners (c16).
- Wealth is permitted up to a ceiling that prevents takeover-scale accumulation (c17).
- The floor for individuals is the birth account plus voluntary support; there is no mandatory welfare (c18, c19).
- Extra funding comes only through a capped, sunset emergency tax (c28) or single-purpose project entities that close on completion (c29).

## Layer 6: Defense

The overall posture is fixed in the constitution; four operational items remain open. Rogue cell enforcement (formerly 6B) now lives in 1C.

**Already decided (from the constitutional layer):**

- No standing army and no universal service. Cell militias for local events; pre-committed cell-to-body agreements with assigned specializations for large conflicts; volunteers first, conscription only at grave trigger points (c26).
- WMD are defined by capability and held in equal custody across cells. Manning is contributed equally across cells, with rotation or contract filling gaps so headcount stays proportional (c24).
- The national security body has no domestic surveillance authority. The body may run an outward-facing intelligence and development service funded by cell contracts (c12, c26).
- A separately elected oversight body handles internal accountability and diplomacy (c13).
